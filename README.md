Project Overview
This project analyzes employee salary data to understand how salaries differ based on gender and employment category.
The dataset includes 474 employees and focuses on three main variables:
Gender
Employment Category
Current Salary
Dataset Summary
Total employees: 474
Females: 216
Males: 258
Variables:
Gender (categorical)
Employment Category (categorical)
Current Salary (numerical)
Key Findings
Salary by Gender
The average salary for females is 26,031, while for males it is 41,442.
This shows that males earn about 15,000 more on average.
Male salaries also vary more, and the highest salaries are significantly higher than those of females.
Employment Category Distribution
Most employees work in clerical jobs (76.6%).
Females are mainly concentrated in clerical positions, while males are more distributed across clerical, custodial, and managerial roles.
Males are also more represented in managerial positions.
Salary by Employment Category
Managerial positions have the highest salaries.
Clerical positions have moderate salaries.
Custodial positions have the lowest salaries.
Managerial salaries also show the greatest variation.
Conclusion
The analysis shows a clear difference in salaries based on gender and job category.
Male employees earn more on average and are more likely to hold higher-paying managerial positions.
This suggests that employment category plays an important role in explaining the salary gap.
